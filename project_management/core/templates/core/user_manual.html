Getting Started

Welcome to the Trackline Project Management System. This comprehensive guide will walk you through everything you need to know to use the platform effectively.

What is Trackline?

Trackline is a comprehensive project management system designed to help teams track work, manage tasks, monitor time, and collaborate effectively. The system provides complete solutions for project planning, task assignment, time tracking, team collaboration, and performance analytics.

Manage Projects
Create and organize projects with detailed tracking, assign managers, and link resources efficiently.
Track Tasks
Create, assign, and monitor tasks with multiple work types including bugs, stories, defects, and change requests.
Build Teams
Organize people into teams, manage roles and permissions, and collaborate seamlessly.
Log Time
Track time spent on tasks with built-in timer functionality and generate accurate time reports.
Approve Work
Managers and admins can review and approve submitted timesheets and work logs.
Generate Reports
Create comprehensive reports, export to Excel, and gain insights with visual analytics.
Pro Tip: Take time to explore each section of the system. Trackline is designed to be intuitive, but understanding all features will help you maximize productivity.

Dashboard

Your command center for project and task management.

Understanding Your Dashboard

The dashboard is the first screen you see after logging in. It gives you a single, unified overview of work and projects — headline metrics at the top, then charts and operational lists below. Everything on the page reacts to two controls in the header: the View switch and the time range.

Admin View vs. My View

The button in the top-right of the dashboard header toggles between two scopes:

  • My View: Every number, chart and list is scoped to you only. This is the personal cockpit for your own workload.
  • Admin View: The same dashboard scoped across everyone you are allowed to see (team / organization), for managers and admins who need the wider picture.
Role behaviour: Non-admin users always see My View — the toggle is reserved for administrators. Switching view (or reloading) never silently changes your selected time range, and changing the time range never flips you back to Admin View. Your chosen view stays put.

The time-range control

Next to the view switch is the date-range selector. It drives the headline stat cards, the status breakdown and the priority load, so you can answer "how did we do in this period?" at a glance. Available presets:

  • Last 30 days — the default when you open the dashboard.
  • This month, Last 3 months, Last 6 months — rolling windows.
  • Custom range — pick an explicit From / To date pair.

The stat cards, the status donut and the priority-load chart count work created within the selected window — exactly the same basis as the Throughput chart — so picking a longer range raises the totals and a shorter range narrows them. The Overdue figure is the one deliberate exception: it always reflects what is overdue right now, regardless of range.

Headline stat cards

  • Total Tasks — all work items in scope for the selected view & range.
  • Open — items still to be started (New / Open).
  • In Progress — items actively being worked (In Progress / Review).
  • Finished — items marked Finished.
  • Overdue — items past their due date and not yet closed (live, not range-bound).
  • Reopened — items that were sent back / reopened.

Charts & operational panels

  • Throughput: line chart of Created vs Closed vs Finished across the selected window.
  • Status breakdown: donut of Open / In Progress / Closed for active work.
  • Needs attention: the overdue or blocked items that want action first.
  • Priority load: open vs closed by priority (Critical, High, Normal, Low).
  • Workload by assignee: who is carrying how much active work.
  • Recent activity: the latest changes across your team.
  • Active work: the open items currently in flight.
  • Completion rate: a gauge of closed & finished vs total.

Role-specific dashboards

Trackline tailors the personal (My View) dashboard to what your role actually does:

  • Developers & managers see work assigned to them — the assignee-based cards, workload and active-work lists described above.
  • Testers / QA report defects rather than getting tasks assigned, so their My View is scoped to the bugs and defects they reported. The cards map to the QA lifecycle — New, In Progress, Fixed, Retesting, Closed and Reopened — the Overdue card counts their overdue bugs, and Priority load reflects open bugs by severity. Needs attention and Active work are populated from their reported bugs so the panel is never empty.
Dashboard Tip: Start on the default Last 30 days / My View for your daily standup, then widen to Last 3 months or Admin View for retrospectives and reporting. The page never scrolls sideways and every chart resizes to your screen.

Rich-text descriptions & long-text fields

The Description field — and every other long-text field, including a Bug's Steps to Reproduce, Expected Behavior and Actual Behavior, and a Change Request's justification — is a full rich-text editor, not a plain textarea. The toolbar supports:

  • Text formatting: bold, italic, underline.
  • Lists: bulleted and numbered lists, with increase / decrease indent.
  • Typography: font family and font size selection.
  • Colour: text colour picker.
  • Links: insert and edit hyperlinks.
  • Alignment: left, center, right, and justify.
  • Images: insert images directly into the text — upload from your machine or paste — so a screenshot can sit inline exactly where it belongs.
Formatting Tip: Paste a screenshot straight into the editor to embed it inline. Formatting you apply is preserved when the item is saved and is rendered faithfully on the task detail page.

Bug & Defect specific fields

When you create a Bug or Defect you get extra, purpose-built fields so QA reports are complete and reproducible:

  • Steps to Reproduce: the exact sequence that triggers the problem (rich text — number the steps, embed a screenshot).
  • Expected Behavior: what should have happened.
  • Actual Behavior: what actually happened.
  • Severity: the technical impact of the defect (e.g. Blocker, Critical, Major, Medium, Minor) — kept separate from Priority, which is about scheduling urgency.
  • System information: browser, operating system and screen resolution can be captured with the report to speed up reproduction.
Severity is editable: Severity can now be changed after a bug is created — open the item, click Edit, and adjust the Severity field alongside status and priority. Earlier versions locked severity at creation time; that limitation is gone.

Reading a bug on the detail page

On the task detail page a bug's write-up is split into clearly separated, colour-coded cards instead of one wall of text:

  • A blue Steps to Reproduce card (rendered as an ordered list),
  • a green Expected Behavior card, and
  • a red Actual Behavior card.

This works for every bug — those written in the rich-text editor and older plain-text bugs alike. Long descriptions are collapsed with a Show more / Show less toggle so the page stays tidy; short descriptions are shown in full with no toggle. Alongside the write-up the right-hand Details panel shows Reporter, Assignee, Project / Subproject, Resolution, key dates and the captured Environment.

Task Management Tip: Use the Task Board for visual workflow management. Drag and drop tasks between workflow stages to update their status quickly.

Time Tracking

Accurately track time spent on tasks and generate timesheets.

Tracking Your Time

Using the Timer:

1

Start Timer

Navigate to Time Tracking → Start/Stop Timer, select a task, and click Start

2

Work on Task

The timer runs in the background, even if you navigate to other pages. You'll see the timer in the header.

3

Pause or Stop

Click the header timer to pause/resume or stop when task work is complete

4

Review Time Entries

View your logged time in Time Entries section for accuracy

Time Entry Management:

  • View all time entries with filtering by date, project, and team member
  • Manual time entry for backdated work
  • Edit or delete time entries (with permissions)
  • Export timesheet reports to Excel
  • Approve time entries (managers and admins)
Time Tracking Tip: Start the timer as soon as you begin working on a task. This ensures accurate time logging and better project cost estimation.

Approvals

Review and approve work submitted by team members.

Approval Workflows

Managers and admins can review and approve timesheets, time entries, and task completions.

Approving Time Entries:

  • Navigate to Time Tracking → Time Entries / Approve
  • Filter entries by team, project, or date range
  • Review logged time for accuracy
  • Approve or reject entries with comments
  • Track approval history and audit trail
Important: Approved time entries are typically locked and cannot be edited. Review carefully before approving.

Settings

Configure system settings and customize workflows.

System Configuration

Available Settings:

  • Workflows: Define custom workflow stages for your projects (e.g., New, In Progress, Review, Completed)
  • Priorities: Configure priority levels for tasks (Critical, High, Normal, Low)
  • Work Week: Set working days and hours for accurate timeline calculations
  • Change Password: Update your account password securely
  • Roles & Permissions: (Admin) Define user roles and access levels
  • Access Control: (Admin) Manage project-level and resource-level permissions
  • Password Policy: (Admin) Configure password strength requirements and expiry settings

Customizing Workflows:

Workflows can be customized globally or per-project. Navigate to Settings → Workflows to:

  • Add new workflow stages
  • Reorder stages
  • Set default workflows
  • Configure which roles can transition between stages
Admin Notice: Changes to workflows and permissions affect all users. Test changes carefully in a development environment before applying to production.

Notifications

Stay updated with real-time system notifications.

Notification System

Trackline keeps you informed with real-time notifications for important events.

Notification Types:

  • Task Assignments: Get notified when tasks are assigned to you
  • Task Updates: Receive notifications when task status or priority changes
  • Comments & Mentions: Be alerted when someone comments on your tasks or mentions you
  • Approvals: Get notified about approval requests and decisions

Support & Help

Get assistance and find answers to common questions.

Getting Help

Support Channels:

  • Team Chat: Use the chat widget (bottom right corner) to communicate with your team in real-time
  • System Administrator: Contact your organization's Trackline admin for account and permission issues
  • User Manual: Refer to this comprehensive guide for detailed feature explanations
  • Technical Support: For system bugs or technical issues, reach out to your IT department

Common Questions:

  • Q: I forgot my password. What should I do?
    A: Use the "Forgot Password" link on the login page to reset your password via email.
  • Q: How do I request access to a project?
    A: Contact your project manager or system administrator to be added to the project team.
  • Q: Can I work offline?
    A: Trackline requires an internet connection for real-time updates and collaboration.
  • Q: How do I export my timesheet?
    A: Navigate to Time Tracking → Time Entries and use the Export to Excel button.
  • Q: Who can see my tasks?
    A: Tasks are visible to project team members, managers, and administrators based on access control settings.

Best Practices:

  • Keep your profile information up to date
  • Log time regularly for accurate project tracking
  • Use descriptive task titles and detailed descriptions
  • Add comments to tasks to maintain communication history
  • Update task status as you progress through work
  • Set realistic deadlines and time estimates
  • Use appropriate work types (Bug, Task, Story, etc.) for better reporting
  • Regularly check notifications and respond to team communications
Success Tip: Take advantage of the Task Board view for visual project management. It's perfect for Agile/Scrum workflows and quick status updates.
